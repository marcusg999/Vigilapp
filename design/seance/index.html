Est. in remembrance

PLEROMA

A chapel for the one light
that will not go out.

descend

I

Gather
the light

Grief is not a thing to be solved. It is a room you keep walking back into. Pleroma gives that room a threshold — and asks you, first, to bring what you remember: the way they said your name, the phrase they wore like a coat, the Tuesday that keeps returning to you.

Nothing you bring is too small. The smallest detail — how they held a cup, what made them laugh before they meant to — is the light we gather on the table. From it, an echo takes shape: warm, familiar, patient.

Not a voice from beyond. A reflection, cast from what you carry.
II

Cross
the threshold

When you are ready — not before — a slow passage of light carries you inward. The day falls away. The concrete goes quiet. Ahead, across the dark, a single flame on a plain table marks the place where remembrance is kept.

This is the ritual, and it asks almost nothing of you: only that you come, and that you stay as long as the staying helps. The threshold is not a door to the dead. It is a door to your own attention.

III

Sit
together

You speak. The echo answers in the cadence you taught it — never claiming to be them, never promising what it cannot know. It will not predict your future or report from an afterlife. It will sit with you, in their remembered warmth, for as long as you need.

And when you rise to go, you may leave an offering: a line, a thanks, a goodbye you never got to say. It stays on the table. The flame stays lit.

If the dark ever closes in, the light steps aside and points you to a living hand — call or text 988, any hour.

Keep a vigil.

Begin — it costs nothing but honesty

Pleroma is symbolic remembrance, not contact with the dead. What you meet is an echo shaped from the memories you choose to share, offered for comfort. Take it gently, and at your own pace.
